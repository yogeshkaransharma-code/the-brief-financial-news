The publication

Good financial news should make sense.

The Brief follows the changes shaping UK banking, insurance and the wider financial-services industry — and explains them without jargon, noise or unnecessary complication.

Why The Brief exists

A new insurance rule, a bank's results or a change in how financial products are sold can affect thousands of firms and customers. Yet useful facts often sit in long reports, technical notices and promotional announcements.

Our aim is straightforward: tell readers what happened, what is known, who may be affected and what still needs explaining. We want each story to stand on its own, with a clear route back to the original source.

What we cover

We focus on UK banking, general insurance and protection, brokers and intermediaries, pensions and employee benefits, financial technology, regulation and major industry developments. A story earns a place because it is timely and useful — not because a company has put out a press release.

How we produce our reporting

Our newsroom uses automated research and generative AI to discover original documents, prepare articles and challenge their factual claims. A separate checking process compares material statements with the source, looks for discrepancies and withholds drafts when concerns remain. There is no routine human approval of each published piece.

Those checks reduce some obvious mistakes, but they do not guarantee accuracy. Models can misunderstand context and primary sources can omit important qualifications. We identify the originating organisation and link to its original material; company assertions are attributed, not treated as independently proven facts.

Freshness and selection

For daily news, our standard is developments from the preceding 24 hours. For the 23 October 2026 relaunch, we also cover developments first announced on 22 October. Earlier events should not be relabelled as new. If reliable evidence is unavailable, we would rather publish fewer stories than pad the edition.

Corrections and accountability

If we discover that an article materially misstates a fact, date, financial figure or regulatory status, it should be corrected or withdrawn and the change explained. We are developing an operational corrections process; at present we cannot promise around-the-clock review, and we do not pretend that we can.

The Brief is not affiliated with the firms or regulators it covers. Our articles are informational, not personal financial, investment or insurance advice.
