Why I started The Brief

Before moving to the UK for my MBA, I spent nearly four years working in insurance. I saw how decisions made inside financial firms affect brokers, businesses and the people relying on them.

I've always felt those stories deserve to be explained in a way that makes sense beyond the people writing the reports. That's why I started The Brief.

I hope you find it useful, whether you've worked in financial services for years or you're just getting your head around it.

Why we're here

There's plenty of financial news. What isn't always easy to find is a straightforward explanation of what has actually changed, who's affected and why anyone should care.

The Brief brings together developments in UK insurance and banking and explains them without the unnecessary fuss. Whether you work in the industry or simply want to understand it, you should come away knowing something useful.

What we cover

Our two desks are Insurance and Banking. We follow the firms, regulators, commercial decisions and everyday developments shaping these sectors, from underwriting and broker distribution to lending, payments and customer outcomes.

Our approach to sources

We draw on official releases, regulatory publications, company statements and established news organisations. We link to the original material so readers can see where the information came from.

Each report is based on attributed material, with checks for publication dates, source availability, UK relevance and duplicate stories. Those checks are not independent verification of every claim, and reports do not routinely receive a separate editorial review before publication.

Company claims and other publishers' reporting are attributed to their sources. We link to the original material, welcome corrections and do not claim to have interviewed people or independently confirmed every underlying statement.

An edition for today

Today's news should actually be today's news. The Brief publishes new reports based on sources dated the same UK calendar day and keeps previous editions in the archive.

We check regularly for new developments. When there isn't anything new worth presenting, we'd rather leave the space quiet than recycle an old headline and call it fresh.

Corrections and accountability

Good information matters, and so does putting mistakes right. If you spot something inaccurate, please send us a correction request with the article and relevant source. We aim to make material corrections clear rather than quietly rewriting the record.

The Brief is independent of the organisations it covers. Its articles are for information, not personal financial, investment or insurance advice.
