Good financial news should make sense.

Insurance and banking shape more of our lives than we might realise. We want the important stories to be easier to follow, without losing the detail that matters.

Why we are here

There is no shortage of financial news. The challenge is finding the story underneath the jargon, the lengthy reports and the endless headlines.

At The Brief, we want you to understand what happened, why it matters and what we still do not know. Whether you know the industry inside out or you are new to it, you should be able to follow along.

What we cover

The Brief has two desks: Insurance and Banking. Broker, MGA, underwriting, claims and insurance-regulation stories belong to Insurance. Lending, payments, markets and banking-regulation stories belong to Banking. We look for developments worth understanding, not just announcements worth repeating.

Where our stories come from

We follow insurers, brokers, banks, regulators and established independent publishers. Our articles link to the material behind the story, whether that is a regulatory announcement, a company statement or original journalism. When a company makes a claim, we say whose claim it is.

Our reporting uses AI-assisted writing from retrieved sources, with automated checks for dates, source integrity and duplicate stories. Individual articles are not separately fact-checked or signed off by a human editor, and these checks cannot establish that every claim is true. We do not claim interviews or first-hand verification that did not happen.

Our writing standard is simple: explain what happened in plain British English, add practical context when the evidence supports it, and leave out speculation dressed up as fact. A small announcement should be a small story. A consequential development deserves more care and context.

Keeping the dates straight

For today's edition, the original source and our article must share the same UK calendar date. We check regularly for new developments, but we will not pass yesterday's announcement off as today's news merely to fill the page.

Past editions remain in the dated archive. Some earlier material carries a verification notice because its source or publication dating has not been retrospectively certified.

Corrections and accountability

We want to get the details right. If an article contains a material mistake, it should be corrected or withdrawn, with the change explained. You can report a possible correction through our public issue tracker. Include the article link and original evidence; do not include private information. Corrections require a checked article revision and a visible notice. We cannot promise round the clock human review.

The Brief is not affiliated with the businesses or regulators we cover. Our articles are for information, not personal financial, investment or insurance advice.
