Standard Life study finds friction hampers customer pension understanding

Three years of behavioural science research by Standard Life indicates that avoidable complexity and effort can stop customers from understanding key pension decisions.

Original news: 2026-10-22 · Published by The Brief: 2026-10-23 · Standard Life

Friction creates barriers to decision-making

A three-year analysis of customer behaviour by Standard Life found that customer understanding is less likely to fail because information is missing, and more likely to break down when avoidable friction, uncertainty and complexity make it harder to identify what matters and what to do next. The provider's "Understanding Under Pressure" report gathers behavioural science research, customer testing, experimentation and journey analysis. Standard Life notes that these barriers, sometimes known as sludge, can be particularly significant for customers experiencing vulnerable circumstances such as bereavement, poor health, financial pressure or major life events.

High clarity ratings mask gaps in comprehension

The research highlights a distinction between whether a communication is perceived as clear and whether customers can successfully use it to make informed decisions. In one test cited in the report, participants gave a pension withdrawal communication an average clarity rating of 4.3 out of 5. However, when asked to explain what could happen to the portion of their pension that remained invested after taking a withdrawal, only 40% were able to do so. Standard Life notes this presents a challenge under Consumer Duty regulations, where delivering good customer outcomes depends on customers being able to understand and act on information in practice.

Redesigned journeys reduce inbound queries

To help firms assess customer communications, Standard Life created a four-question framework focusing on where to start, why it matters, what to notice and what to do next. In a live randomised controlled trial of redesigned pension withdrawal communications, customers using the revised materials were 2.5 times more likely to complete the process. Inbound calls fell from 40% to 24%, while requests for further information reduced from 18% to 5%. In a separate redesign of its online bereavement notification service, more than 80% of customers successfully uploaded a death certificate during their first interaction.
