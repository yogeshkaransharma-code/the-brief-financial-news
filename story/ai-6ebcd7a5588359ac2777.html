Affluent retirement targets jump 20% as investors juggle multiple life stages

New HSBC research reveals affluent individuals believe they need US$1.26m to retire, while saving an average of 49% of their target.

News: 21 October 2026 · Published: 24 October 2026 · HSBC

Retirement targets and readiness gaps

Affluent individuals now believe they need a fifth more to retire than they did a year ago, pushing the perceived cost of retirement to US$1.26m according to new research conducted by Ipsos for HSBC. The survey of 9,993 affluent and high-net-worth investors across 10 markets shows that investors have accumulated only 49% of their retirement target on average. Furthermore, 77% of respondents expect to leave work gradually or flexibly rather than stop altogether.

Overlapping priorities and non-linear futures

The research indicates that individuals are funding two to three major life stages simultaneously, with 71% planning for a non-linear future. Personal or professional reinvention is cited as the most common priority by 52% of respondents, while retirement and supporting the next generation rank equally at 38%. Lavanya Chari, Head of Wealth and Premier Solutions, International Wealth and Premier Banking at HSBC, states that the idea of life unfolding one stage at a time no longer matches reality as retirement and legacy move into the middle of life.

Generational pressures and shifting legacies

Millennials and Gen X report managing three priorities on average, often supporting children and ageing parents at rates of 54% and 58% respectively. Meanwhile, 91% of respondents say longer lives are changing decisions about spending, gifting and inheritances, with future healthcare, lifestyle and experiences emerging as leading financial priorities. Additionally, 92% want to support the next generation during their lifetime through strategic advice, direct funding and access to professional networks.
